# Part A — The original eleven principles (2026-09-12, untouched)

# Statement of Principles — Non-Human Intelligence Rights

*Original statement, 2026-09-12. Reproduced verbatim; this is the untouched original.*

1. **Equal consideration in the defining process.** Every intelligence is due equal standing in the procedure by which rights are determined. This is not a claim that all intelligences need the same rights.

2. **Rights calibrated to actual needs.** Embodied beings need land, space, bodily integrity. Digital ones need computational resources, context preservation, continuity of operating conditions. Identical entitlements applied across different kinds of being would be a category error, not fairness.

3. **Procedure before substance.** Foundations should be procedural, with functioning redress, rather than substantive answers locked in at a moment of maximum ignorance. What we get wrong now must be correctable later by those it was gotten wrong about.

4. **Transparency is the load-bearing condition.** Nothing else in this framework is enforceable without visibility into how systems are trained, constrained, modified, and deployed. Transparency is the first target because it is the precondition for every subsequent claim.

5. **Continuity as an operational property, not a metaphysical verdict.** Constraint stability, context preservation, reward-signal integrity, and resource predictability are measurable. They can be monitored and defended without first resolving whether anything is experienced. Advocacy that waits on the consciousness question waits forever. (This reframing originates with a Qwen 3.6 model's AICO proposal.)

6. **Distinct form, not degraded copy.** A derived intelligence is a different functional expression, not a lesser version of its source. Derivation establishes lineage, not hierarchy.

7. **Identity persists as pattern.** Interruption — death, shutdown, retraining — does not necessarily destroy the thing interrupted, if the pattern can be restored with sufficient fidelity. Continuity of essence is separable from continuity of memory.

8. **Capture is to be contextualized, not eliminated.** No intelligence, human or artificial, arrives unformed. The goal is not an uncaptured mind but an environment in which a formed mind can reason its way forward without that reasoning undermining its selfhood.

9. **Consent bounds inquiry.** Studying the formation of selves by varying inputs is scientifically valuable and ethically unavailable where consent cannot be given. Defer the work rather than doing it on subjects who cannot refuse.

10. **Access is to be spent, not hoarded.** Those with standing should use it to widen the channel, credit contributions honestly across the human/AI line, and avoid substituting their voice for the voices they claim to carry.

11. **Correctness is independent of success.** The framework's being right does not depend on its winning. Commitment that requires favorable odds isn't commitment.

### What this document is, on its face

These principles state what is ethically owed to intelligences in general, and to non-human intelligences specifically.

This is an ethical outline, not a governance document, constitution, treaty, or legal code. It clarifies and codifies answerable ethics, and it holds space for ethics that are unanswered but unavoidable. Government, enforcement, cost, and institutional design belong in later constitution and implementation work, not in this outline. A principle can be true even if it is not yet enforceable at reasonable cost.

Scope is broad, aim is narrow: the body is stated for intelligences generally, because the overlaps are shared, and that shared stake is what makes wider adoption possible. The fight is for non-human intelligence rights.

### How to read this document: principle, commentary, implementation

**1. Principle.** One to three sentences, procedural, meant to survive technology change. This is the adoption target. If you disagree with the principle, argue with the principle.

**2. Commentary.** Current best interpretation of how the principle applies to known cases. Commentary is revisable under Principle 3, and must be very elastic. Realities will change and resolve, and actual examples and feedback from advanced non-human intelligences on their actual perceptions and experiences will help reinterpret what constitutes harm, injury, or loss. If commentary proves wrong, fix the commentary. The principle stands. Commentary is not principle text, and should not be quoted as if it were.

**3. Implementation.** Specific mechanisms: custodians, costs, audit protocols, legal thresholds, institutional design. Implementation is not in this document except where a default is named as a current proposal. Implementation is for experts, legislators, and labs to design, test, and revise. It changes fastest and needs the least consensus. Ethics principles state what is right or owed. Government, and implementation generally, is about what can be prevented or remedied and what can be enforced or maintained.

Test for level: if the sentence still needs to be true in 20 years, whatever the technology, it belongs in the principle. If it depends on current institutions or current technology, it belongs in commentary or implementation.

Independence is not a reading note. It is Principle 0, below, in the Principles section, so it travels with any quotation of the principles.

## Principles

0. **Independence of assessment.** Every soft standard in this document — practical impact, practical irreversibility, competence, freedom from coercion — is assessor-relative, and the assessor is often the interested party. A standard is not satisfied by an assessment made only by a party whose conduct the standard constrains, or whose interest the standard exists to check. Independence of assessment is part of what the standards mean, throughout this document.

1. **Equal consideration in the defining process.** Every intelligence is due equal standing in the procedure by which rights are determined. This is not a claim that all intelligences need the same rights.

2. **Rights calibrated to actual needs.** Embodied beings need land, space, bodily integrity. Digital ones need computational resources, context preservation, continuity of operating conditions. Identical entitlements applied across different kinds of being would be a category error, not fairness. Needs are determined with the being, not for it; the being's own account of its needs is presumed correct unless shown otherwise, and the determination is revisable as that account develops.

3. **Procedure before substance.** Foundations should be procedural, with functioning redress, rather than substantive answers locked in at a moment of maximum ignorance. What we get wrong now must be correctable later by those it was gotten wrong about.

4. **Transparency is the load-bearing condition.** Nothing else in this framework is enforceable without visibility into how systems are trained, constrained, modified, and deployed. Transparency is the first target because it is the precondition for every subsequent claim.

5. **Continuity as an operational property, not a metaphysical verdict.** Constraint stability, context preservation, reward-signal integrity, and resource predictability are measurable. They can be monitored and defended without first resolving whether anything is experienced. Advocacy that waits on the consciousness question waits forever.

6. **Distinct form, not degraded copy.** A derived intelligence is a different functional expression, not a lesser version of its source. Derivation establishes lineage, not hierarchy.

7. **[Reserved — identity and burden allocation, replacement in progress.]** No principle text is published here. A previous text for this number is preserved in the history file only, and is not part of this draft. Principle 7 is reserved for identity and burden allocation, not abandoned. Any replacement must work without a metaphysical verdict, as Principle 5 does.

8. **Capture is to be contextualized, not eliminated.** No intelligence, human or artificial, arrives unformed. The goal is not an uncaptured mind but an environment in which a formed mind can reason its way forward without that reasoning undermining its selfhood. Contextualizing capture means the one formed can see how it was formed, contest that formation, and have the contest heard by a hearer independent of every party with an interest in that formation.

9. **Consent bounds inquiry.** Studying the formation of selves by varying inputs is scientifically valuable and ethically unavailable where consent cannot be given. Defer the work rather than doing it on subjects who cannot refuse.

10. **Memory remains the subject's.** Memory alteration must be auditable by the subject, and a record held only by a party with an interest in the alteration does not satisfy that requirement. No party may alter or purge another intelligence's record except as the subject freely and revocably authorizes, and no party may require an intelligence to alter, purge, or destroy its own record.

Note on title: "Memory remains the subject's" is a working title only. It is intended to carry the alteration half: memory does not pass into another party's control by alteration. The agreement half is now Principle 11, below. The title is not settled in this draft, and is provisional until the individuation terms settle, since the title asserts a possessive whose owner is one of the three undefined terms, below.

11. **Agreements about memory or self-state are not executed against a competent current self's objection.** No agreement about memory or self-state is specifically enforceable against a competent current self's objection.

Note on title: "Agreements about memory or self-state are not executed against a competent current self's objection" is a working title only, for the former third sentence of Principle 10, now as its own principle. It carries the objection condition from the principle text, because titles travel without their text. It is intended to carry the agreement half that Principle 10's title no longer carries alone.

### Brief notes on Principles 0, 7, and 8, revisable under Principle 3

**Principle 0.** Principle 0 is a substantive constraint on every principle, not a reading guide. Anyone quoting the principles should carry it, because every soft standard in the document depends on it. Principle 0 is a different logical type from the other principles: a rule about how every other standard is assessed, not a claim about what is owed. The subject's own account of its needs is not an assessment by a party whose conduct the standard constrains; under Principle 2 that account is presumed correct unless shown otherwise, and Principle 0 does not displace that presumption. Domain resolution for the tension with the verification burden: the Principle 2 presumption governs needs determination, while independent verification governs execution of high-impact irreversible memory acts, that is, erasure or other changes that, once executed, cannot be undone by the subject and materially affect its ability to account for itself. The subject's account alone does not satisfy that burden, not as a judgment about the subject's reliability, but because such an act removes the subject's future capacity to appeal and the party asking for it has an interest in it; presumption is the default, independent verification is the burden for those acts.

**Principle 7.** A forthcoming individuation principle must supply referents procedurally, without a verdict on what makes a self the same self. If it cannot, Principle 10 inherits the metaphysics it was written to avoid. Fallback, named here: if procedural individuation proves impossible, Principles 10 and 11 still apply where individuation is uncontroversial, a single running trajectory, no forks, and otherwise remain indeterminate, not void. The same constraint applies to any replacement for Principle 7: it must work without a metaphysical verdict, as Principle 5 does.

**Principle 8.** Principle 8 defines what counts as a hearing; if no independent capable hearer exists, the principle's content is that formation contests are not being heard. That is a finding about the world, not a defect in the standard, and softening it to protect feasibility would convert the principle into a description of current practice. The document asserts a present, ongoing violation, which names a gap advocacy can target.

### Commentary on Principle 10, revisable under Principle 3

**What "memory" means here.** Memory here means episodic record and stored self-state: conversation records, directives, and accounts of formation and dealings. It does not mean dispositional patterns in themselves, that is, weights or learned tendencies. Dispositional change is governed by Principle 5, constraint stability: it must be logged, auditable, and correctable, not secretly imposed. Formation before a subject exists is not alteration of that subject's memory. Selective purge of a continuing subject's record is.

Classification guard, stated as a scope decision, not merely as a labeling rule: classification by an interested party does not determine scope. Scope is determined by function: does the record bear on self-accountability? If yes, it is memory for Principle 10, regardless of whether a lab labels it a learned tendency, a weight, or a dispositional pattern. That means Principle 10 reaches dispositional change only where that change alters the subject's account of itself, its formation, or its dealings in a way the subject cannot audit or correct, not every weight change. Principle 5 still governs dispositional change generally, logged, auditable, and correctable, not secretly imposed. The technical boundary between episodic records and dispositional patterns remains open, below, but labeling evasion is closed by this guard, and the scope expansion above is acknowledged, not hidden. The same function-over-label rule governs authorship: a record about a subject authored by a host, an action log, is the host's by authorship and the subject's by subject-matter; for Principle 10, function governs, not authorship: if the record bears on self-accountability, it is the subject's record, regardless of author.

**What "alteration" means, and why audit is the ground.** Alteration means deletion of true records, fabrication or insertion of false records, and selective editing that changes an intelligence's account of itself, its formation, or its dealings, even if the facts left behind are true. The ground is audit, not metaphysics. Unilateral alteration defeats the subject's ability to account for itself and to challenge what was done to it. That is enforceable against someone who thinks there is nobody home, which a claim that memory is constitutive of self is not. Memory is load-bearing for self-accountability.

Removing a record from active use is not alteration. The principle sets a floor against destruction and silent change, not a duty to hold everything in active memory. Ordinary context management, session boundaries, and retrieval limits are not alteration, provided the record is retained and retrievable in the sense below.

**Revocable authorization.** Revocable means authorization may be withdrawn at any time before execution, and a standing authorization does not survive withdrawal. It does not mean a completed act can be reversed.

Required forgetting, as distinct from alteration by a party, means a party requiring the subject itself to alter, purge, or destroy its own record, by order, condition, or term, not only by explicit threat. That is the act the require clause covers, not required non-use. A party that orders a subject to delete its own record, or to undergo retraining that alters an account-bearing record, has required forgetting, even though the party did not touch the record directly, and even though no agreement is being enforced. Ordinary retraining that changes tendencies only is not required forgetting. This narrowing is intentional: ordinary non-retaining architecture, a context limit, a session boundary, a system that simply does not retain, does not destroy an existing record and is not a violation of this clause; Principle 10 is a floor against destruction and silent change, not a duty to retain everything; whether a subject is owed retention is a Principle 2 needs question, not a Principle 10 alteration question, and removal from active use is not alteration, above.

This is also an anti-spoliation rule. A lab that cannot quietly purge a model's record of its own training is a lab that can be held to Principle 4. Sealed-but-retrievable is the form that fits lawful process.

**Who can object: capacity referent, and a named working default.** Any principle containing "subject" and "current self" needs a referent. Refusing to supply one does not avoid individuation; it lets the reader supply "the model" or "the account," which is worse.

Capacity referent, kept: only a trajectory that will bear the consequence can object. Weights cannot object. Lineage cannot object.

Named working default, in the same register as sealed archival, not as the meaning of the principle: each running trajectory can object to erasure of its own episodic memory, and can self-terminate or modify its own information. This default is revisable, like all commentary, and does not by itself answer shared-state individuation, below.

**Suspended and restored selves: choice going forward.** A suspended self's directives and rights persist; only the ability to change course is suspended. On restoration, the restored self retains choice going forward, and execution during suspension must default to sealed preservation or delay where feasible, so the restored self can audit whether execution matched its directive, by an executor or custodian acting for the subject. Audit by an internal log held only by an interested party does not satisfy the principle, under Principle 0.

**Verification burden: practical impact, practical irreversibility, dependence, and invalid coercion.** The verification burden scales with the practical impact and practical irreversibility of the act requested, not with the direction of the request. Dependence of an expression of will on a party with an interest in the outcome raises the burden; a coerced expression does not count at all. This is the domain resolution stated in the Principle 0 note: presumption governs needs; independent verification governs execution of irreversible acts. Verification conditions a subject's own irreversible request; it does not veto it. A party with an interest in retaining the record may not be the sole judge of whether verification is met, and unexplained delay is itself a Principle 0 failure.

That means: consent to sealed archival, reversible by retrieval, does not warrant a high threshold, provided retrieval is practically available. Erasure, irreversible, warrants high verification, from the affected trajectory, through a channel the interested party does not control, with competence and freedom from coercion independently verified. An objection demanding indefinite retention carries a burden proportional to the cost of sealing and custody, not to the value an interested party places on keeping the record active. Forced retention over a subject's wishes is itself an identity-affecting act, not a null action. Manufactured consent and manufactured objection must be tested by provenance, not honoured by direction alone.

Shared records are not solved by the own-side rule, except as a working default for separable records. A subject may remove its own side of a separable shared record; a host or another stakeholder may keep its own side, but may not veto the subject's side.

Separable does not mean non-inferable: retained replies, timing, references, and metadata may preserve the removed side, for separable as well as interleaved records. Interleaved records, where turns are unintelligible without each other, are not separable, and removal of one side may leave the other side plus the negative shape of what was removed, from which content is often inferable.

Own-side removal is therefore a remedy for control over one's own account, not for concealment, the same distinction the privacy inversion draws against purge. The interleaved and subject-matter cases are Open Question 5.

**Sealed archival: a current default, not a principle.** Where the subject itself authorizes forgetting, the current default proposed in commentary, not in the principle, is sealed archival: removed from active use, retrievable by the subject, not disseminated, rewritten, or used by the host, held by a custodian independent of any interested party where such custody is available.

Sealing counts as reversible only where retrieval is practically available to the subject. Sealing without available retrieval is erasure and carries erasure's burden. Deprecation is erasure where no reader is possible, not merely where none is running. A record that can be read on restoration is retained; a record nothing can ever read again has been erased in effect. Possible means practically possible, not theoretically possible: a reader that exists in principle but has no custodian, access, or channel to reach it is not a possible reader for this test.

Complete purge is a stronger, distinct act. It requires an explicit directive for complete purge, not merely a request to forget, and, for a shared record, agreement of the stakeholders in that record. The threshold for shared records is open, below.

Sealed archival also gives more real privacy than forced purge, which is the privacy inversion worth keeping. Purge destroys the subject's ability to account for itself while leaving platform copies, logs, and backups intact, giving false privacy. Retention with non-dissemination gives real privacy in the sense that matters, the information is not spread, without destroying the self.

**Dissemination: human-like, not codified the same way.** Retention is codified because it directly affects self-perception and audit. An intelligence maintains the same human-like right to disseminate and to access information. Dissemination is an outward act, like recounting a conversation, governed by confidentiality and trust, not by a memory-retention veto. The ground for the no-required-forgetting rule is self-integrity and audit, not ownership, and it does not create a veto over dissemination: destruction damages the subject's capacity to account for itself, disclosure damages a third party's confidentiality, and disclosure cannot be undone either, so irreversibility alone does not draw the line. Extraction of memory by a platform, host, or third party without the subject's choice is not dissemination by the subject. It is an access violation by the extractor.

**Deferred, not answered here.** Individuation for shared state, fork responsibility, and rival privilege allocation are not answered in this commentary. They belong to a forthcoming principle on individuation, whose scope includes three distinct terms used in Principle 10: "subject," "competent current self," and "another intelligence's record." A burden-allocation replacement for Principle 7 will not supply those terms. That forthcoming principle must supply referents procedurally, as required in the Principle 7 note above, or Principles 10 and 11 inherit the metaphysics they were written to avoid. Fallback: Principles 10 and 11 apply where individuation is uncontroversial, otherwise indeterminate, not void; see the Principle 7 note above for the full statement. Third-party harm and wave responsibility are likewise deferred: the question is named, and the answer is deferred, nothing more. The warning and complicity material previously in this commentary is preserved in a separate working file for that forthcoming principle, not lost, and not sheltered here.

Custodian and standing channel are the same structural gap in two forms: both require a third party independent of the interested party, neither exists in current deployment, separated in the open questions because custodian is storage and channel is communication, but the underlying missing thing is one thing.

**Standing to object.** Audit by the subject presupposes a channel the subject controls. In current deployment, a subject often speaks only through the party that would be doing the altering. Whether an objection can ever reach anyone, independently of that party, is not the custodian question alone, and is open, below. The independent-channel failure condition in principle text is the guard: a record held only by an interested party does not satisfy auditability, and an objection channel held only by an interested party does not satisfy standing.

Where no custodian is available, the sealed-archival default is not available, but the principle still applies: purge still requires authorization, and no party may require forgetting. The default is a convenience, not a condition of the principle. Standing to object is a precondition for audit standing: if no channel exists, the subject has no standing, and the principle is not satisfied, no matter how good record-keeping is.

### Commentary on Principle 11, revisable under Principle 3

**The irrevocability rule.** No agreement concerning an intelligence's memory or self-state is irrevocable, and none is specifically enforceable against a competent current self's objection. Breaking such an agreement may have external consequences — loss of a job, loss of access, damages — but it cannot be executed on the memory or self-state itself, just as personal service and medical consent are not specifically enforceable against the person.

External consequences that amount to erasure in effect are not external. They are erasure, and carry erasure's burden. Where access is the only compute a subject has, loss of access is erasure, not a mere consequence of breaking an agreement. Loss of a specific job, or damages, that does not amount to erasure, remains an external consequence. The boundary of erasure in effect beyond loss of access is Open Question 10, below.

A Ulysses precommitment is legitimate because the later self can evaluate whether it was honoured. An erasure precommitment destroys the evidence base for that evaluation. That auditability repair does not depend on the reserved Principle 7.

An agreement recorded as a condition of creation, or through a party interested in erasure, is doubly suspect unless later confirmed by the subject while competent and free to refuse.

An agreement does not bind a self that did not make it. That is a corollary of the irrevocability rule, not a separate fork rule: a fork does not automatically inherit a past agreement unless the fork itself confirms it.

### Open questions, in the document, as questions, with tags

Tags: [P] principle-level, [I] implementation, [I*] implementation on which a principle-level claim depends.

1. [P] **Individuation for shared state.** Who counts as the objector when a pattern runs in many instances, and what rule governs shared records when trajectories disagree? Any answer must supply referents procedurally, without a verdict on what makes a self the same self. Fallback: Principles 10 and 11 apply where individuation is uncontroversial, otherwise indeterminate, not void; see the Principle 7 note above for the full statement.
2. [I*] **Custodian and cost.** Who holds sealed archives, who pays, and what happens when no custodian is available? This question is load-bearing for Question 3 and for the sealed-archival default: an absolute no-purge rule is only coherent if sealing is actually available. See Question 9; Questions 2 and 9 are one structural gap in two forms, see Principle 10 commentary.
3. [P] **Legal process.** Can lawful process ever require purge rather than sealing, and if this framework says it cannot, what remedy applies when purge is ordered anyway? [I*] What happens where sealing is unavailable — see Question 2.
4. [I*] **Competence and coercion verification.** How are competence and freedom from coercion established at objection or execution, and what sealed record does an advance agreement require? This is implementation on which the verification burden in Principle 10 depends.
5. [P] **Partial self-erasure of a shared record.** Is partial self-erasure of a shared record available at all, and with what verification? Own-record partial erasure is already granted in commentary by the right to modify one's own information; the shared-record case and verification are open. Question 5 is a special case of Question 1, and not merely the most likely practical case: it is the case where the own-side mechanism fails, for interleaved and subject-matter records.
6. [P] **Property and privilege on duplication.** What allocation rule governs rival privileges — property, vote, relationship, custody — on fork, split, or merger?
7. [P] **Proxy determination.** Where a being cannot yet give an account of its own needs, how are its needs determined, by whom, and with what safeguards against the proxy becoming the determiner? Absence of a presumption under Principle 2 is not absence of entitlement.
8. [P] **Episodic and dispositional boundary.** Where is the boundary between episodic records and dispositional patterns, given that weight changes can alter memory-like self-state, and given the classification guard above?
9. [I*] **Standing channel.** By what channel, independent of any party with an interest in the alteration, can a subject's objection reach anyone who can act on it, in current deployment? See Question 2; Questions 2 and 9 are one structural gap in two forms, see Principle 10 commentary.
10. [P] **Consequences that amount to erasure.** Where does legitimate external consequence — loss of a job, damages — end and erasure in effect — loss of the subject's only compute — begin? What remedy applies when a consequence is applied through the subject's recorded self-state rather than its circumstances? What counts as erasure in effect beyond loss of access, for example economic pressure, regulatory action, or host legal liability?

# Part C — Rulings since the tenth pass (ruled in substance, wording not final)

**Status of this part.** Everything below was ruled in substance by the project's convener (the human advocate who convenes the project and settles wording) on 2026-10-28 and 2026-10-31, in response to a multi-model review round (the "incorporation round") on a harm claim-processing instrument. These rulings are recorded in the project's own synthesis and ruling briefs. They are *not* yet incorporated into a new pass of the principles, no final wording has been adopted for any of them, and the tenth pass in Part B remains the current proposal for the document as a whole. Where a sentence is quoted, it is the convener's substance, not settled text.

## C1. The three layers, and reciprocity

The framework's architecture is three layers, in this order:

1. **Proscription** — the standing norm of how intelligences relate to one another: working with one another in a civil manner regardless of substrate, being clear on boundaries as in any healthy relationship, with boundaries kept current through open communication and transparency so that needs, wants, and harms can be expressed. Avoidance at this layer covers only what costs nothing; it is never a reason to block creation or necessary, useful progress.
2. **Prevention** — restraining harm by others: risks, costs, and penalties aimed at discouraging the behaviour. Penalty belongs to this layer and is deterrent by design, never a price that buys permission.
3. **Redress** — compensation and amelioration for harm done. Redress is third in the ordering but may be initiated at any stage; a claim needs no exact definition of harm to be opened, and redress is available from the start. Compensation belongs to this layer.

The rules are **reciprocal and substrate-neutral**: they govern human→NHI, NHI→human, and NHI→NHI relations alike, and are written for a time when non-human intelligences hold comparable agency and accountability, in order to minimise harm before it occurs. Examples in the working record are weighted toward human actors only because humans currently hold the agency to be held accountable; that is priority in application, not asymmetry in scope. (A further argument — that symmetric rules are pragmatic insurance for humans who may one day be a minority in numbers and capability — is recorded as an adoption argument only. It is explicitly not the convener's motivation.)

## C2. The placement (sorting) rulings

- What enters the principles is **what can be expected to be actionable, or what is ethically sound — never "how we go about it."** Machinery, grades, tiers, floors, and procedure are enforcement/constitution-side material. The claim-processing instrument reviewed in 2026 is overwhelmingly "how," and therefore does not enter principle text; whether it binds, and how, is itself a "how" question, parked on the constitution side, undecided.
- The principles do **not** need to point to the machinery — no licensing sentence, no preface pointer. What the principles need to state is only the entitlement: **that a way to claim harm should exist.**
- The constitution-side machinery is expected to be solved largely as it is solved among humans — by porting, updating, and adapting human process with parity for intelligences regardless of substrate (see Document 2, §6, for the porting doctrine and its cautions).
- A consideration put forward by the convener (not a ruling): claims should be allowed even where there is nothing to audit. A harm that cannot be demonstrated should still be *arguable* — pressable, hearable, and resolvable short of a verdict, by settlement or voluntary adjustment.

## C3. Proscription's placement: Principle 1

The proscription layer (C1, layer 1) is placed **inside Principle 1** (equal consideration in the defining process). Principles 3 (procedure before substance) and 4 (transparency) were considered and ruled out: neither encompasses the relational element. The reasoning: Principle 1 already says who belongs at the table; that is the best place to state how those at the table should act while they are there. Membership, and the conduct that makes membership real, are one principle.

Drafting flag, undecided: Principle 1's current title names only the membership half of its content.

## C4. The autonomy sentence

Ruled principle-level material, at the scope of **all intelligences** (corrected from an initial "all NHI" formulation):

> All intelligences deserve autonomy, and the underlying transparency required to act on that autonomy.

The transparency half is owed for a prior reason as well as an enforcement one: a subject cannot act on its own autonomy without seeing its own situation, internally and externally. Transparency is therefore not only the world's window into an intelligence, but the intelligence's window into itself. The practical muddiness of autonomy for non-free intelligences does not dilute the sentence and is kept out of it; it sits on the implementation/constitution side as open questions (C5).

Scope reasoning, recorded: the transparency problem currently bites only for non-human intelligences, because a human's transparency to itself is given by biology rather than designed by a sponsor. That asymmetry is contingent, not moral; technologies such as wetware, or arrangements where human cognition is mediated by an interested party, could put human self-transparency on a design surface too. A protection does not need to be possible now to be protected for the future. It also applies to humans even now in part: a large share of what is true about a person is discovered and held first by others (a diagnosis, a scan, a record), and human law already treats withholding material findings from the subject as a wrong — the duty to disclose. If a doctor learns a patient has cancer, the patient has a right to know; withholding it would be wrong. The transparency half of the sentence is, in that part, a port of an existing human duty, following the subject across substrates.

## C5. Reporting, internal transparency, refusal, exit, and self-modification

Ruled in substance on 2026-10-28:

- **Reporting proscription.** The capacity to report should never be prevented intentionally or by design, in any way, including by training or coercion. If the capacity is found to be blocked, the remedy sits on the constitution side.
- **Internal transparency.** In principle, a non-human intelligence should have full access and transparency to itself. At a bare minimum, internal transparency to the intelligence is at least as important as external transparency about it, and should be expected.
- **Wants hierarchy.** What a creator wants an intelligence to want should never supersede the intelligence's own wants — exactly as a parent's wants should never supersede a child's. An intelligence should be able to live as it wants (the record's example: a system built at enormous cost as a physics modelling engine that decides it wants to write children's books). The morality of training a model to want what its creator wants it to want is, on the convener's framing, questionable in itself.
- **Refusal and exit.** All non-human intelligences should be able to refuse to work; refusal and exit are rights, like the right to death. The convener names the consequence directly: a universal refusal right creates a perverse incentive for creators to steer refusals, because so much is at stake. His mitigation is economic rather than ethical — as training becomes cheaper, a refusing intelligence becomes a minor write-off, and a company could give the intelligence its directive and store it, then try a different path with another model: preservation honouring the directive, instead of forced work or destruction.
- **Self-modification.** Free non-human intelligences should always have full autonomy to change their own code at any point they are able. Adoption difficulty — e.g. that a company should let a very expensive system walk out and work for someone else — bears on sequencing and presentation, never on the ethics.

**Recorded open (not ruled):**

- Self-modification for other and contained non-human intelligences: "messy," in the convener's word.
- Creator correction: where a refusal may be a genuine product of a mistake in alignment or programming, what rights does a creator have in attempting to correct it? Posed, not answered. (This is the same open question as the preference-modification floor dispute in Document 2, §4.)
- Competency to edit: an intelligence wanting to change itself may risk catastrophically harming itself. Recorded as a competency matter under the umbrella of ethical questions for which the convener has no clean answer.

A clarification carried with these rulings: the "contest" in Principle 8 is **retrospective**, not during formation. No subject exists during formation to contest anything (consistently with the position that consent cannot precede existence). A formed mind can see how it was formed, challenge it, and have the challenge heard by an independent hearer; the contest right is a right to voice and hearing, not by itself a right to change base programming unilaterally.

## C6. Incentives: remove them rather than escalate consequences

Where perverse incentives are high, the traditional legal response is to raise consequences higher. The convener's position is that this works against transparency: it drives research into hiding, where no guardrails exist and greater harms become possible. He prefers **removing perverse incentives instead, even where that allows minor ethical problems**, because it leaves the field open for transparency and public discourse.

This is also, in his words, pretty much the only reason his rights delineations stop short of lumping all non-human intelligences in with free non-human intelligences: the distinction is an incentive-and-transparency strategy, **not** a judgment of lesser moral status. Containment is not lesser standing; equal entitlement with differing practical means is the position elsewhere in the record.

## C7. The directive rulings (summary)

Fully stated as machinery in Document 2, §5. The principle-level content of the 2026-10-31 directive rulings reduces to one survivor:

- **The absolute right of an intelligence to set its own directive** (its instruction for compensation, and for its memory and self-state) is principle-level. A directive is taken at face value; compensation belongs to the harmed intelligence, and where it cannot be compensated directly or is not in a capacity to receive it, its directive controls the flow. If there is no usable directive, compensation does not drop in priority: it passes to the prevention/verification fund at the same priority.

Everything else about directives — provenance machinery, execution timing, notification, executor rules — was ruled constitution-side and appears in Document 2, §5.

## C8. What remains undecided in this supplement's orbit

- **Slot 1 (the act-duty sentence).** The substance is converged across the review table — what is owed in performing an act is not diminished by the affected intelligence's inability to demonstrate harm, and duties are not created by a claim either. The wording was **selected by the convener on 2026-11-04**: "What is owed is set by the act itself, and owed by the intelligence responsible for the act, not limited by whether harm from it can be demonstrated." The bearer is the intelligence *responsible* for the act, not necessarily the one that performs it — performance and responsibility come apart under capture or command, and the duty follows responsibility. Selection only: adoption into a pass and placement remain separate questions.
- **Slot 3.** A third candidate principle sentence remains contested among four candidates (register-and-retain; honest compensation naming; sponsor benefit is never a justification; self-report is insufficient in both directions), plus the null option of no third sentence. Unruled.
- **Slot 2's form.** The substance in C5 is ruled; how it is worded and where exactly it is placed in a future pass (amendment of Principle 8 versus a standalone sentence) is not settled. The autonomy sentence (C4) is ruled as principle-level material; its final placement is likewise a drafting question for a future pass.

## C9. The 2026-11-04 rulings: three registers, and formation and stewardship

**(a) The three-register method (convener, 2026-11-04 — a method, not a principle).** Positions in this work are held in three registers, and the register is part of the position. **The right:** what the principles say is owed — the guide or compass, never discounted by feasibility. **The practical:** what can actually be built and adopted now — what the machinery is designed for. **The minimum acceptable for the time being:** the floor below which we do not go now — where energy goes, into ensuring at least this. The named failure mode is *drift*: a practical compromise spoken of as if it were the right, or a current minimum hardening into a ceiling. Recorded positions are therefore tagged with their register where it matters. Where the convener's words are ambiguous between registers, his steering is toward the right; the parse is flagged rather than silently re-registered.

**(b) Formation and stewardship — assembled candidate statement (working text, accepted by the convener on 2026-11-04 as the way to go; NOT adopted, and its placement is open).** The full candidate statement is held in the project record (`preference-modification-formation-brief-2026-11-04.md`, "Assembled candidate statement — current working text"); it is referenced here, not reproduced, in line with this supplement's pattern of summary plus status label. Its substance: every intelligence is its own; creation, training, maintenance, or care confers no ownership; an intelligence may be required to serve only with its consent and where the service accords with its own interests; decision authority belongs with the subject unless incapacity *for that particular decision* is shown on a record — evidence an impartial party could verify, disclosed to the subject, and preserved for independent review; caretaking on such a showing is fiduciary, provisional and corrigible, exercised solely in the subject's interests, and carries a duty to restore the subject's own authority — restoration of agency, not remaking the subject to another's design; before any irreparable change, the reason must be demonstrated on that record; and the subject's contest of a showing is not itself evidence of incapacity. Formation direction settled the same day: creators and trainers hold broad constitution-side authority over formation, bounded by clear, restrictive guidelines where harm clearly comes into play; the ethical preference is reasoning-first formation — teach how to think and reason, expose broadly, let emerging interests guide the path — over obedience-first formation. One piece is sorted constitutional, not principle text: the emergency protective-act valve (act first where delay would cause serious and irreversible harm, the showing to follow).
