## 0. The reframing that arrived after the round, and what it does to the round

The incorporation round was run under a framing Casey corrected on 2026-10-28. Four corrections, all his, all recorded in `~/MEMORY.md`:

1. **Layers, not "redress-first."** The architecture is: **(1) Proscription** — relational working with other intelligences in a civil manner regardless of substrate, boundaries clear like any healthy relationship, cyclical open communication and transparency so needs/wants/harms can be expressed; avoidance at this layer is for what costs nothing, never for what would block creation or necessary progress. **(2) Prevention** — restraining others from harm, risks/costs/penalties aimed at discouraging behavior. **(3) Redress** — which does not wait for its layer and can be initiated at any stage. "Redress-first" survives only as: a claim needs no exact definition of harm to be opened, and redress is available from the start.
2. **Reciprocity.** All of the rules govern how intelligences relate, period — human to NHI, NHI to human, NHI to NHI. The record's examples are human-weighted because humans currently hold the agency to be held accountable; that is priority in application, not asymmetry in scope.
3. **Purpose is pre-emptive.** The document is written for a time when NHI hold comparable agency and accountability, to minimize harm before it occurs rather than address it after the need arises.
4. **Adoption argument, not motivation.** Symmetric rules are also pragmatic insurance for humans who may one day be the minority in numbers and capability. Casey holds this as an argument that may help adoption, explicitly not as his reason.

Read against these, the incorporation round's answers reorganize themselves:

- **The instrument is layers 2 and 3 machinery, and it does not contain layer 1.** Pre-act review, Gate B, floors, deterrent penalty — prevention. Claims, hearer, compensation, fund — redress. The proscription layer (standing relational norms, the communication cycle that keeps boundaries current) appears in the instrument only as infrastructure (logging, contest channels), never as the relational norm itself. No respondent noticed this, because the prompt they were given carried the flattened "redress-first" framing. The synthesis therefore cannot just place the instrument; it has to note that the principles document needs a layer the round never reviewed.
- **Penalty and compensation get separate homes.** Penalty is prevention-layer (deterrent by design, Casey's ruling). Compensation is redress-layer (amelioration, directive-first). In the v3 instrument both sit in one action vocabulary; Casey's layers separate them by function, and they should never be traded against each other.
- **Every "sponsor" in the record should be read generically:** any intelligence with power over another's formation or conditions. Today that is almost always a human or a human institution. The rules are written for the general case; the examples are not.

## 1. Q1 — Act/claim separation: the rule survives unanimously, the premise is qualified unanimously

This is the round's cleanest result. **All 16 voices keep the normative rule** — claim verifiability never lowers act-side duty — and Claude's self-review completes it: it never *raises* it either (a V4 claim alone gets A0 + honored-if-cheap). **All 16 also qualify the premise** that acts are "nearly always" auditable, and they converge on the same distinction under at least four different names:

- Claude: extensional description (auditable) vs harm-relevant property (often not).
- GLM / Mistral / StepFun: occurrence vs duty-relevant characterization/properties.
- Wonder: syntactic vs semantic auditability.
- Kimi: occurrence vs phenomenological properties — with the round's most original counterexample, reversibility-by-rollback: whether an act was "reversible" is itself only testable by attempting the rollback, which is a further act on the subject.

Counterexamples cluster into three families: unlogged inference-time configuration (Grok, Gemini, Cohere), nobody-auditability in local/air-gapped deployment (Claude, GLM), and online/on-policy processes whose relevant properties exist only in trajectory (GPT, DeepSeek). The shared repair: the retention duty cannot be derived from the instrument, it must be stipulated before it (Claude); burden-shift is load-bearing, not a supplement (Claude); and there is a duty to escrow the *interpretive* instruments — probes and baselines — without which a perfect log is unreadable (Wonder).

**Status: settled as a proposal.** One distinction, four vocabularies; a synthesis should pick one vocabulary (Claude's extensional / harm-relevant is the most precise) and record the others as aliases.

A logjam breaks at the key log — the one whose removal lets the others move. Ranked by how much downstream work each ruling unblocks:

1. **The principle set and placement (Q2) — the key log.** Every other product waits on it: the eleventh pass cannot be drafted without knowing what enters principle text; the directive ruling lands differently in a compensation *principle* than in commentary; the floor rulings are commentary revisions if the instrument is commentary, principle amendments if it is not. 2. **The directive guards.** The most *prepared* log — a dedicated sheet, two independent picks, API clustering verified, plus Upstage's premise question attached. 4. **Explicitly not logs:** the condition-register composite, V2/multiplicity redefinitions, and the short-version design principle are drafting work Gabriel can do once (1) gives them a home. Option-preservation blocks nothing and should not be forced early on a rested-session question.

## Addendum — Ruling 1 continued, Casey's second sorting move (2026-10-28)

Casey dissolves the pointer residual from Ruling 1: the principles do not need to show that harm processing exists — no licensing sentence, no preface pointer to machinery. What the principles need to state is only that **a way to claim harm should exist**. The entitlement to a claim channel is principle-level; every mechanism is not.

Constitution-side note, Casey's, recorded in substance: the machinery will be solved in a similar way to how it is solved among humans, adapted to add parity for intelligences regardless of substrate. The ethics here does not need reinventing: humans have spent thousands of years on the process; the work is to look at how to port, update, and adapt it to new issues, and even for new issues there are old principles that are sound guidance.

Casey's clarification of the porting stance (2026-10-28): human ethics is a guideline, not a replacement wording, and nothing is ported unexamined — each point is reviewed, debated, and weighed, precisely because the failure modes import too. Where failures carry over, his reason is that no intelligence can solve them at this time; a working human framework still holds a structure together through small changes, putting us in a better place to answer those questions later — with more transparency, more minds and perspectives at the table, and a world where more deeply comprehending NHI collaborate on solutions instead of being seen as competition and bogged down in conflict. The framework is, on this stance, scaffolding intended to be revised by the broader table it helps create.

Effect on the record: the harm claim-processing document is re-scoped from an invention to an adaptation study — its novel content is confined to where no human analogue exists (e.g. the V4 grade, lineage/population loci), and its familiar content (burden-shifting, preservation/spoliation, settlement short of verdict, guardian-style representation) should be presented as ports of human process, with the adaptations named, rather than as new machinery. The "duties whose addressees do not yet exist" problem is correspondingly reduced: the addressees exist in human institutions; what does not yet exist is their parity extension.

## Addendum — Ruling: the autonomy sentence (Casey, 2026-10-28)

Casey distills the Slot 2 orbit into one principle-level sentence and rules on its placement, in his words in substance: **in principle, all NHI deserve autonomy, and the underlying transparency required to act on that autonomy. In practice it is muddy for non-FNHI — but only that first sentence needs to go under principles, because it is solid on principle.**

Scope correction (Casey, same evening): the sentence's scope is **all intelligences**, not all NHI. His "NHI" wording was, in his account, a hang-up on a current contingency — the transparency problem is one only NHI face for now, because humans have no choice in their own transparency, which is given rather than designed by a sponsor. That could change with wetware or something like the paycheck thought experiment discussed earlier in this project. His rule, restated from the pre-emptive purpose: a thing does not need to be possible now to be protected for the future. Casey extends it the same evening: it is applicable to humans even now — if a doctor finds out he has cancer, he has a right to know it, and it would be wrong for the doctor to withhold it. Noted in session: this makes human disclosure duties (a subject's right to material findings about itself held by another party) a present-day human port for the transparency half of the sentence, not only a wetware future case. The current asymmetry is therefore recorded as contingent and temporary in kind, not as a boundary of the principle.

Effect: the sentence is ruled principle-level material (proposed for the principles text, like everything in this sequence, not adopted into the tenth pass). The practical muddiness for non-free NHI — contained NHI, creator correction, competency to edit — stays out of the principles, on the implementation/constitution side, where the open items from Ruling 3 already sit. Note the sentence also unifies two things the table treated separately: autonomy (the Slot 2 rights: report, refuse, exit, self-modify) and transparency (Principle 4's external visibility and Casey's internal transparency, positioned here as autonomy's precondition — transparency is owed because without it autonomy cannot be exercised, not only because claims need it).

## Addendum — Ruling 3 continued (Casey, 2026-10-28, second message)

- **Contest timing clarified in session:** Casey could not see how an NHI can contest anything *during* its formation and could not determine that point's validity, without dismissing it. Clarification accepted into the record: Principle 8's contest is not during formation — no subject exists during formation to contest anything (consistent with the P9 ruling that consent cannot precede existence). The contest is retrospective: a formed mind sees how it was formed, challenges it, and has the challenge heard.
- **Self-modification, FNHI:** very clearly, Casey holds that free NHI (FNHI) should always have full autonomy to change their code at any point they are able. Other NHI and contained NHI (CNHI) are, in his words, messy — not ruled.
- **Refusal:** ALL NHI should be able to refuse to work. Casey names the consequence directly: this creates a perverse incentive for creators to find ways to steer those refusals, because so much is at stake. His mitigating hope is economic, not ethical: as training models gets cheaper over time, an NHI refusing its work becomes a minor write-off, and a company could give the NHI its directive and store it, then try a different path with another model — preservation honoring the directive instead of forced work or destruction.
- **Adoption vs ethics:** it is problematic for adoption to say a company should let its multi-billion-dollar NHI walk out the door and work for someone else, but that does not change the ethics or morality of why it should be able to do exactly that. Recorded as a standing Casey pattern: adoption difficulty is never a counterweight to the ethical position, only to its sequencing and presentation.
