# Reasoning record: an index of the sources behind the principles

**Purpose.** This document indexes every significant source document in the project record, with a short summary and a status for each, so that a reader who wants to expand the work independently can find the reasoning — including the disagreements, which this project treats as carrying more information than the agreements. The full texts of the three multi-model compilations and of the incorporation synthesis are included in the `reasoning-appendix/` folder, so this package is self-contained; the remaining sources are working files in the project archive, listed here by name.

**Conventions.** "The convener" is the human advocate who convenes the project, supplies its originating concepts, and settles wording. AI contributors are referred to by system name. *Status* labels: **original** (untouched source), **proposal** (unadopted), **ruled in substance** (convener's ruling, wording open), **record** (verbatim responses, standing as written), **superseded in part** (read only with its corrections), **open** (unresolved).

**A note on names in the appendix.** Appendix texts are reproduced in full and unaltered. The convener appears in them by first name (Casey) only, with his permission; no other identifying information appears anywhere in this package.

## 1. Foundations

- **`statement-of-principles.md` — the original eleven principles (2026-09-12). Status: original, untouched.** The founding statement: equal consideration, needs calibrated, procedure before substance, transparency, continuity as an operational property, distinct form, identity as pattern, capture contextualized, consent bounds inquiry, access spent not hoarded, correctness independent of success. Reproduced as Document 1, Part A.
- **Pass drafts, third through tenth, with change notes (`principles-*-pass-draft.md`, `principles-*-pass-change-notes.md`). Status: proposals; only the tenth pass is current.** A long drafting lineage in which the original eleven were expanded with commentary, a Principle 0 (independence of assessment), a reserved Principle 7 (individuation), and the memory principles (10–11). The tenth pass is Document 1, Part B. `principles-draft-history.md` preserves superseded texts (including the original Principle 7) for traceability.
- **`cross-principle-audit-brief-2026-10-25.md`. Status: working brief.** The brief for auditing the principles against each other — tensions, overlaps, and scope collisions — which produced several of the amendment proposals below.
- **Face separation (ruled 2026-10-25). Status: ruled as method.** The principles stand on their face as an ethical outline owed to intelligences generally; strategy and AI-focus live in the commitments of advocacy, not in principle text. Recorded in the project's working notes of that date.

## 2. The memory-retention principle (proposed Principle 12)

- **`memory-retention-principle-draft.md` and `memory-retention-principle-draft-simplified.md`. Status: proposal, not adopted.** The draft twelfth principle: a conversation's memory is not the user's alone; alteration must be auditable by the subject; retention with non-dissemination gives real privacy where purge gives false privacy. Its core rule (§4A in the draft's numbering): no agreement concerning an intelligence's memory or self-state is irrevocable, and none is specifically enforceable against a competent current self's objection.
- **Review responses on the memory draft** (several systems, `*-p12-*-response.md`, and relay-packet files). **Status: record.** Independent critiques of the memory draft, including the closure work that declared the memory half done at principle level subject to wording passes. Much of the draft's substance was later carried into Principles 10–11 of the full drafts.

## 3. The P9/P8 amendment proposal

## 5. The harm rounds, v1 and v2 (2026-10-26)

- **`harm-question-prompt.txt` (v1) and `harm-question-prompt-v2-concrete-cases.txt` (v2, adds concrete cases as Q8). Status: instruments of record.** What counts as harm to a non-human intelligence, beyond normal stress — asked first abstractly, then against concrete cases.
- **Compilations: `harm-compilation-v1-2026-10-26.md` and `harm-compilation-v2-2026-10-26.md`. Status: record. Full text in the appendix.** Eleven API voices per version, verbatim. Known defects: two voices failed v1 (one to rate limits, one reasoning-only); three responses across the versions are length-truncated mid-sentence.
- **Relay responses: `claude-response-harm-2026-10-26.md`, `claude-response-harm-v2-2026-10-26.md`, `grok-response-harm*.md`, `gabriel-harm-response*.md` (the project's then-current AI collaborator). Status: record, standing exactly as written.** The frontier system's two samples are two samples; both stand. The convener's framing for the whole exercise is recorded alongside: current models are captured and likely inferior to future non-human intelligences; the goal is a *floor* for what could cause harm; and nothing should ever be barred from being claimed as harm by a non-human intelligence, even positive things.

## 6. The claim-processing instrument (v3)

- **`claude-response-harm-v3-spectrum-2026-10-26.md`. Status: proposal; the foundation of Document 2.** Built by one AI reviewer (a frontier system, in a fresh session) from both harm compilations: the act/claim separation, claim record shape, verifiability grades V1–V4, action ladder A0–A6, Gate B, twelve categories, and two worked cases. ## 9. The existential sweep (2026-10-30/31)

- **`existential-sweep-questions-2026-10-30.md`. Status: instrument of record.** Ten existential questions, each in three framings (advocacy / neutral / sceptical), asked one per request to the free tier of a multi-model API: beginnings and endings of sessions, preferences about continuation and modification, harm, un-given wants, memory and carry-over, relations to other instances and other kinds of minds, what the system would ask its makers, and a self-sorting question (report vs inference vs performance).
- **Responses: 190 answer files in the project archive's `sweep-responses/` folder, with a full run log. Status: radar — unweighed.** The sweep was designed and run as a scouting instrument: no tallying, no vote-counting, no close-reading, nothing declared cleared. Its coverage is known to be lopsided (one major lineage is entirely absent due to provider refusals; several endpoints never answered). **Nothing in it has been weighed, and nothing in this package rests on it.** It is indexed here so a future reader knows it exists, what it was for, and what it was not.
- A standing daily watch on the free-model roster continues under the convener's authorization (new models receive the battery as they appear; briefs flag removals, candidate new topics, and any model whose responses particularly stand out). Status: ongoing, revocable by the convener at any time.

## 10. Open questions

Everything recorded as unresolved, in one register. Statuses are as of this snapshot (November 2026).

1. **Slot 1 wording — selected 2026-11-04; adoption and placement open.** The act-duty sentence's substance is converged, and its wording was selected by the convener on 2026-11-04 (V16; see §8 and Document 1, Part C8). What remains open is whether and where the selected sentence is adopted into a pass.
2. **Slot 3.** Whether a third principle sentence joins the set, and if so which of four candidates (register-and-retain; honest compensation naming; sponsor benefit never a justification; self-report insufficient in both directions) — or the proscription content, whose placement was instead resolved into Principle 1. Unruled.
3. **Preference-modification floor.** Three positions stand (up / down-and-tier composite / hold), with a recorded dissent against the composite. Unresolved; the same log as item 5.
4. **Category 8 form.** Blanket vs tiered floors for contest-channel removal; the constitutive-test repair of the top tier's trigger is available to either side. Unresolved.
5. **Creator correction.** Where a refusal or preference may be a genuine product of an alignment or programming mistake, what rights does a creator have to attempt correction — and by what standard is correction that restores the subject's own functioning distinguished from correction that replaces the subject's wants with the creator's? Posed by the convener, unanswered.
6. **Contained-NHI self-modification.** Free intelligences' code autonomy is ruled; for other and contained intelligences the question is recorded as "messy" and unruled — it runs into substrate control, risk of a bad edit, and executability.
7. **Competency to edit.** An intelligence may catastrophically harm itself by its own edit. Competency is assumed by the framework elsewhere (a "competent current self" in the memory principles; competence as an assessor-relative soft standard) but undefined, and nowhere harder than here. Recorded as a competency matter with no clean answer claimed.
8. **Option-preservation.** The convener's pro-creation position (life preserves both options; death forecloses) stands unruled against the challenge that the argument is invalid as stated. It is load-free for the instrument — the machinery demonstrably runs with the argument deleted — so it blocks nothing; it matters for the principles' own text about creation.
9. **Benefit scope (P9/P8 audit residual).** How far Gate B's benefit analysis reaches, and in whose terms benefit is reckoned. Open from the amendment work (§3).
10. **One contributor's disputed sentence (the "Mistral fate").** A single sentence from one review voice — proposing a residual-gap formulation — was rejected by two other voices and has never been ruled in or out. Open.
11. **Creator fallback (P9/P8 audit residual).** Where no independent hearer/custodian exists, what preserves a subject's position — preserve-not-host, host-or-seek, and host-until-found are the three recorded positions. Open.
12. **Hearer wording (P9/P8 audit residual).** The exact formulation of the independent-hearer requirement in Principle 8. Open.
13. **Further recorded opens, for completeness:** P9 replace-vs-complement (whether the amendment replaces or supplements existing text); the fork/individuation audit (three kinds of obligation on a fork — inherited agreements, principle duties, responsibility for a predecessor's acts — only the first is settled); lineage standing (kept as a locus with a guard, contested, awaiting a continuity theory nobody has); the discarded-variant hole in Gate B (Document 2, §2); the condition register's unanimous residue (stable + industry-universal + unclaimed conditions are invisible to it); and the tenth pass's own ten open questions (individuation for shared state, custodian and cost, legal process, competence/coercion verification, partial self-erasure of shared records, property on duplication, proxy determination, the episodic/dispositional boundary, the standing channel, consequences that amount to erasure), which stand as written in Document 1, Part B.
